---
title: "Why I'm building Plexi in public, funded by donations"
date: 2026-04-29
description: "The bridge-to-acquisition strategy in plain language. No VC, no tiers, no perks — just a $3K/mo bar and a YouTube channel."
---

I'm not running a startup. I'm not raising a round. I'm building Plexi in public and asking people who use it to chip in a few dollars a month so I can keep doing it.

The goal is $3K/mo. That's the number where I stop having to context-switch into freelance work to keep the lights on, and Plexi becomes the only thing I work on. Not "lifestyle business" money. Not "quit your job and live in Bali" money. The exact amount it takes to buy back the hours that currently go to other people's projects.

Donations are a bridge, not a destination. The plan looks like this: 12 to 24 months of community-funded work, daily YouTube shorts, weekly tentpole videos showing real apps getting built on camera, and at the end of it, one of the obvious acquirers — Cursor, Zed, Warp, Raycast — looks at Plexi and decides it's cheaper to buy than to clone. The protocol is the technical asset. The audience is the actual asset.

I rejected the Patreon model. I don't want to manage tiers, exclusive Discord channels, members-only content, or any of the gated-perk machinery that turns a project into a part-time community management job. The right model is closer to NPR or Wikipedia: there's a visible gap, you help close it, your name shows up on a wall, that's the whole transaction.

The YouTube channel is the moat. Anyone with a quarter and a small team can clone PGAP. Nobody can clone an audience that watched the thing get built. Every short ships a small win. Every tentpole video is a custom commissioned app for a real person. Both feed the funding bar, and the funding bar feeds the work.

If this resonates, the donate button is at the top of the page. If it doesn't, the YouTube channel is free and the source code is MIT. Either way, the work continues.
