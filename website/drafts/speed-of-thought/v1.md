I have loved AI for far longer than it's been a problem. Only recently was I allowed to really dive in. Only recently was it practical to live amongst an agent swarm and feel fully competent — as somebody with ADHD — to feel like a computer moving at the speed of thought. It's been an incredibly addicting ride, and I've accidentally created the app that helps me do that.

A lot of what's holding back the potential of people-and-agents collaboration is not the agents or the humans, necessarily. It's that we're trying to foster AI growth by duct-taping it to the side of software designed primarily for human use. If we really want to make the most of this revolution, we have to flip the entire model on its head. The separation between "this is a human's job" and "this is an AI job" needs to dissolve. We need an ecosystem where collaboration is seamless — maybe even natural.

Over the past few years, I've been exploring what that landscape might look like. In the past few months, I've had the pleasure of personifying that idea as Plexi.

---

<!-- PLEXI ORIGIN — keep this tactile and pragmatic, not hype -->

I was using Ghostty's separate tabs since the start of the TUI revival. My screen logs were showing Ghostty usage through the roof. I thought: if this is going to be the app I'm spending most of my hours in, I might as well spend some time getting the most out of it.

It wasn't until I saw a Theo clip on Theo.gg — he was talking about Zed or something like Cmox al that I even considered the idea that someone could improve on the humble terminal. I'd used everything:

- Mac Terminal
- VS Code integrated terminal
- Zed
- Warp
- Ghostty

I genuinely had no plan. I couldn't imagine how one could ever improve on the humble terminal, let alone me — someone with less than a year of actual experience as a developer.

Then I realized: there has got to be a cleaner way to do this. What does it mean to get the most out of an interaction with AI? You need two things:

- Predictable, customizable output
- Predictable, robust input

Everything else is baggage.

Upgrading the terminal experience wasn't just interesting — it seemed unavoidable if I was truly going to get the most out of a session. Get the most out of my tokens. (Weeps in Anthropic credits.)

<!-- THE BIGGER VISION -->

The fact that we've really only ever had two and a half systems for driving a computer at a fundamental level — and that it's taken this long — is genuinely terrible. For technology to be this far along and for there to be so few options in how we actually drive our own machines. We've just settled for so many things, for so long.

There's no reason any living person shouldn't be comfortable using a computer to do almost whatever they want, almost instantly. Especially not now.

I have this heart-wrenching feeling that my mom spends maybe 10% of her time in front of a laptop actually doing something. The other 90% she's just fighting buggy, confusing, unintuitive, ugly software that does no justice to the beauty of computing.

I have this delusion — perhaps a delusion — that given affordable technology already available, everyone could be up toward 90%. I've done it for myself. I've found a way to make my computer move at the speed of thought through UX and heavy AI integration. I want to solve this for everyone.

That means:

- Reliable, robust on-device voice-to-text
- Reliable, robust on-device AI (lightweight LLMs)
- Software that will never update your apps without telling you, without your explicit permission

And beyond that: there's no reason to be using other people's software anymore. It's never going to exactly fit your needs — and how could it? It doesn't need to anymore. You can interact with your computer however you want. We should have an ecosystem that reflects that. For some reason, we still don't.

Plexi is my plunge toward that future. It's not feature complete. But I'm going to pour every ounce of energy I have into this project for as long as this life will let me.

Not because I'm getting paid. Not because I need to prove a point. Not because I need a portfolio project.

Because I cannot stop. And I can't imagine ever wanting to. And I have never felt like this before.
