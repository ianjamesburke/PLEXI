Only recently was I allowed to make that dream a reality. It's been an incredibly addicting ride, and I've accidentally created the app that helps me do that.

A lot of what's holding back the potential of people-and-agents collaboration is not the agents or the humans, necessarily, but the fact that we're trying to foster AI growth by duct-taping it to the side of software designed primarily for human use. If we really want to make the most of this revolution, we have to flip the entire model on its head. The separation between "this is a human's job" and "this is an AI job" needs to dissolve, and we need an ecosystem where collaboration is seamless and maybe even natural.

Over the past few years I've been exploring what that landscape might look like. In the past few months, I've had the pleasure of personifying that idea as Plexi.

<!-- PLEXI ORIGIN -->

I was using Ghosty's separate tabs since the impetus of the TUI revival. My screen logs were showing me that my Ghosty usage was through the roof, and I thought: if this is going to be the app I'm going to be spending most of my hours in, I might as well spend some time getting the most out of it.

It wasn't until I saw a Theo clip on Theo.gg where he was talking about Cmox that I had even considered the idea that someone could improve on the humble terminal. I had had the pleasure of using everything I've used:

- Mac Terminal
- VS Code integrated terminal
- Zed
- Warp
- Ghosty

I genuinely thought I had no plan. I couldn't imagine how one could ever improve on the humble terminal, let alone me, someone with less than a year of actual experience as a developer.

I realized there has got to be a cleaner transition to this. What does it mean to get the most out of interaction with AI? You need:

- Predictable and customizable output
- Predictable, robust input

Everything else is baggage. I realized not only was upgrading the terminal experience wildly interesting, but it seemed unavoidable if I was truly going to be getting the most out of a session, getting the most out of my tokens. (Weeps in Anthropic credits.)

<!-- THE BIGGER VISION -->

I want, like me and others who share the passion, the sheer beauty and awe that is personal computing. The fact that we really have only ever had two and a half systems for driving these things in a really fundamental way is terrible. For technology to be this far along and for there to be so few options in terms of how we drive our own technology is terrible. We've just settled for so many things over the years.

There's no reason that any living person shouldn't be very comfortable using a computer to do almost whatever they want, almost instantly. Especially not now. I have this heart-wrenching feeling that my mom spends maybe 10% of her time in front of a laptop actually doing something, and the rest of the time she's just fighting buggy, confusing, unintuitive, ugly software that does no justice to the beauty of computing.

I have this delusion (perhaps a delusion), but given affordable technology already available, everyone could be up toward 90%. I've done it for myself. I've found a way to make my computer move at the speed of thought through UX and heavy AI integration, and I want to solve this problem for everyone.

That means:

- Reliable, robust on-device voice-to-text
- Reliable, robust on-device AI like lightweight LLMs
- Software that will never update your apps without telling you or without giving you explicit permission

And assuming that you don't already just make and use your own apps, because that's also something at the forefront of the interface. There's no reason for us to be using other people's software anymore. It's never going to exactly fit our needs, and how could it? It doesn't need to anymore. We live in a world where you can interact with your computer however you want, and we should have an ecosystem that reflects that, but for some reason we still don't.

Plexi is my plunge toward that future. It's not feature complete yet, but I'm going to pour every ounce of energy I have into this project for as long as this life will let me.

Not because I'm getting paid, not because I need to prove a point, not because I need a portfolio project, but because, for whatever reason, I cannot stop and I can't imagine ever wanting to. And I have never felt like this before.
